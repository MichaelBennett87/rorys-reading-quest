## Editorial review

Every story has a clear conflict, escalating middle, turning point, consequence, and resolution. Beginning, middle, and ending evidence was reviewed for its contribution to the supported theme. No story states its keyed theme as an ending moral. Supported themes are complete thoughts, not one-word topics, plot summaries, commands, or static traits.

The supported theme in each guide is clearly stronger than its topic-only, summary-like, unsupported-moral, and plausible-but-insufficient distractors. A careful reader cannot support a competing visible answer with two or more strong story details. Checkpoints distribute evidence across the plot and require at least one beginning-middle-end mapping plus a bounded two-part evidence dependency.

Character choices, consequences, conflict, and turning points support theme development without making character development the primary scored construct. Perspective, narrator point of view, poem form, informational analysis, unrestricted writing, and FAST practice remain outside the pack.
