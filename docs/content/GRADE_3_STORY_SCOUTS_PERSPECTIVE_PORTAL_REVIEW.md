## Perspective-guide review

Every passage tracks two named characters responding to a shared situation. Every character has at least two resolved evidence references and multiple evidence kinds. Each guide includes a text-supported comparison and motivation statements; the pack contains four `different`, two `partly-similar`, and one `similar` comparison. Three perspective-change records connect an earlier view, a meaningful plot event, and a later view.

Dialogue, action, thought, feeling, noticing, and choice evidence were reviewed against the source sentence IDs. Checkpoint characters include dialogue plus action or thought. Both sides of every comparison have independent source evidence; no viewpoint is invented beyond the story.

## Construct boundaries

- Feeling-only: PASS. An isolated emotion is never keyed as a complete perspective explanation.
- Trait-only: PASS. A character quality is never substituted for a viewpoint toward the shared situation.
- Narrator point of view: PASS. Narration position appears only as a bounded distractor or explicit distinction, never as the key.
- Author perspective: PASS. No item asks learners to infer or score the author's personal viewpoint.
- Theme and character development: PASS. These may be story context but are not the scored construct.

## Editorial and semantic review

All seven stories are original, child-safe Grade 3 literary passages. They use reasonable motivations rather than villain/hero framing, trauma, politics, or absurd opposition. Every prompt, key, distractor, explanation, evidence reference, hot-text segment, table mapping, and two-part dependency received blind solution, authored-key comparison, and adversarial review.

Two source-sentence wording defects were corrected so their Word Help targets occur naturally. One checkpoint multiselect factory was tightened to remove a second defensible evidence choice. The table factory was reduced to a bounded two-row/three-option form so `use_each_once` retains one genuinely unused narrator distractor. No final answer key, explanation, evidence, or evaluator defect remains.

## Word Help, accessibility, safety, and provenance

Each passage owns exactly four useful targets. The five-stage sequence remains Look at the Pattern, Break It Apart, Hear the Parts, Hear the Word, and Hear the Sentence. Targets reconstruct their words, resolve to their sentence, do not reveal the keyed perspective, and require neither audio nor microphone access.

The pack preserves keyboard operation, visible focus, non-color-only selection/result states, accessible multiselect/hot-text/table/two-part controls, reduced motion, responsive layout, Save and Exit, and active-quest protection. The stories are original repository-authored DRAFT material; no commercial worksheet, FAST passage, protected character, private child detail, or remote content was used.
