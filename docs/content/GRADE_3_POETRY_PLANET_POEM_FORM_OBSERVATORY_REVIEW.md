## Form accuracy review

- Free verse: both examples use intentional lineation and are not defined as poetry that can never rhyme.
- Rhymed verse: the two examples use different intentional rhyme organizations; the teaching does not imply that all rhymed verse is AABB.
- Haiku: the example has three lines and an observational focus. Manual syllable review supports 5-7-5 for this authored classroom example only; the teaching explicitly rejects 5-7-5 as a universal haiku law.
- Limericks: both examples have exactly five lines, child-safe playful situations, defensible AABBA end rhyme, and no advanced meter claim.
- Rhyme review: every keyed rhyme relationship was read manually; no answer depends on a disputed pronunciation.
- Form ambiguity: each poem has one clearly strongest classification among the learner-visible options.

## Instructional and question review

- All four required forms appear in instruction and checkpoint transfer.

## Word Help, safety, and accessibility

- Each poem has exactly four support targets.
- Word Help preserves Look at the Pattern, Break It Apart, Hear the Parts, Hear the Word, and Hear the Sentence/context.
