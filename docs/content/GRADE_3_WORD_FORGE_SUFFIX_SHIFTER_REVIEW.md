## Suffix and transformation review

| Suffix | Target count | Authored transformation |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `-ness` | 4 | adjective to noun |
| `-ment` | 4 | verb to noun |
| `-er` | 3 | verb to noun naming a person or thing that performs the action |
| `-ful` | 4 | noun/base to adjective |
| `-less` | 3 | noun/base to adjective |
| `-ly` | 4 | adjective to adverb in the authored sentence |
| `-able` | 3 | verb to adjective |
| `-y` | 3 | noun to adjective |

The provisional `-tion` family was replaced with `-y`. Common Grade 3 `-tion` examples generally require a nontransparent base boundary or spelling-change instruction, which conflicts with this phase's transparent-construction rule. The substitution preserves eight common families, Grade 3 word-analysis scope, and a defensible noun-to-adjective branch.

Teaching and explanations use qualified language such as "can" and "in this word." They do not claim that all `-ly` words are adverbs or all `-er` words name people.

## Morphology, reading chunks, and Word Help

Every meaningful analysis reconstructs `base + suffix = derived word`. Every authored reading-chunk sequence independently reconstructs the same written word. The checkpoint targets `colorful`, `breakable`, and `washable` deliberately show different boundaries:

- `color | ful` versus `col | or | ful`
- `break | able` versus `break | a | ble`
- `wash | able` versus `wash | a | ble`

This makes the distinction visible rather than merely tagging it. Word Help preserves the five accepted stages: suffix highlight, authored reading chunks, separated chunk speech, natural whole word, and source sentence. Browser `SpeechSynthesis` remains optional and cannot guarantee perfect isolated-phoneme production.
