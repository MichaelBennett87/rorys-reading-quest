## Editorial and instructional review

The twenty-eight targets comprise ten metaphors, nine personification targets, and nine hyperboles. Metaphor and personification appear across prose, poetry, and informational formats; hyperbole appears across literary prose and poetry. Each target has one best classification, exact source and context evidence, a meaningfully different literal reading and figurative meaning, and a context-supported explanation. Informational figurative wording supplements rather than replaces factual explanation.

Metaphors use direct comparison rather than primary `like` or `as` constructions. Personification assigns genuinely human action or quality to nonhuman subjects and does not misclassify ordinary animal behavior. Hyperbole is deliberate, clearly nonliteral exaggeration with a unique realistic meaning. Simile, idiom, alliteration, tone, mood, theme, summary, author comparison, vocabulary benchmarks, composition scoring, Grade 4 analysis, and FAST timing remain outside the pack.
