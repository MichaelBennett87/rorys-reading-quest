## Specificity review

- Each specific purpose statement names a concrete informational goal.
- None of the statements collapse to a generic `to inform` label.
- Each statement differs from the topic and from the central idea language used in earlier phases.
- The purpose remains a full thought, not a one-word label.

## "To inform" overbreadth review

- The pack does not rely on `to inform` as the accepted answer.
- The authored questions ask for the text's specific purpose.
- Distractors keep the learner focused on the whole-text goal, not a generic PIE label.

## Topic-versus-purpose review

- The topic is a short phrase naming what the text is about.
- The purpose is the author's reason for writing the text.
- Checkpoint items contrast those two ideas directly.

## Central-idea-versus-purpose review

- The pack does not score central-idea mastery.
- Purpose statements stay distinct from the earlier Central Idea Center language.
- Any central-idea-looking distractor is used only as a contrast choice.

## Detail-versus-purpose review

- Individual details support the purpose but do not become the purpose by themselves.
- The questions keep purpose as the whole-text goal rather than one isolated fact.
