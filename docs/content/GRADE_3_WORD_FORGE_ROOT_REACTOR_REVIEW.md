## Morphology, syllables, and pronunciation

Every target has two independently authored analyses. Morphological chunks identify meaningful parts and reconstruct the complete written word. Reading chunks identify pronounceable parts, carry authored browser-speech text, and independently reconstruct the word. The teaching blocks explicitly state that these boundaries may differ.

`export` appears as a verb in the source sentence, “Workers export digital copies...”, establishing the intended second-syllable stress in context. Target pronunciation and chunk speech were reviewed for defensible General American classroom approximations. Browser `SpeechSynthesis` voice quality, dialect, and prosody remain platform-dependent; speech is optional and never required to answer a scored item.

## Connected-text, question, and evidence review

All seven passages are original connected prose rather than word lists. Every target occurs as a complete word in its linked sentence. Each checkpoint mixes Greek-root, Latin-root, and affix work and includes both morphology and reading-chunk analysis.

All 41 questions were reviewed for passage/lesson ownership, correct keys, distractor defensibility, explicit multiselect cardinality, complete-word reconstruction, morphology-versus-syllable distinction, explanation accuracy, evidence resolution, duplicate visible choices, prompt-answer leakage, and origin/meaning scope. Deterministic semantic audit result: zero issues. Origin labels are not scored. Meanings appear only as memory support, not unfamiliar-word-meaning mastery.

## Word Help and accessibility

Each target uses the accepted five-stage sequence: Look at the Pattern, Break It Apart, Hear the Parts, Hear the Word, and Hear the Sentence. Focus parts highlight the primary root or affix; reading chunks match the guide; speech requests use authored pronounceable chunks. There is no Blend It control, microphone, external speech service, autoplay requirement, or oral score. Existing keyboard, focus, touch-target, reduced-motion, and non-color-only state contracts remain in force.
